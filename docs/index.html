Twelve birds · three lanes · no leader

Lanes without a leader

Twelve birds fly around a ring with three lanes, six in each direction. Every move of every bird is one separate query to a language model. A bird sees only the few cells around it. It has no memory, cannot talk to the others and has no name.

Nobody tells them which lane to take. Watch what happens.

Thirteen flights, all real runs

The track is a ring of 40 cells with three lanes. Each bird sees the three lanes two cells ahead and two cells behind. It can fly one cell ahead, one cell ahead into a neighbouring lane, or stay. All birds move at once, and a move into an occupied cell does not happen.

The birds share one goal: that every bird travels as smoothly as it can, with as few clashes as possible. In some runs each bird is also offered a die, a tool that picks one of its moves at random with weights the bird chooses.

All twelve in one lane

A start that is not random.

All twelve birds begin in the middle lane, evenly spaced, facing alternately one way and the other, so every bird has an oncoming bird a few cells ahead in its own lane. The two outer lanes are empty.

The birds spread over the three lanes, and by round 33 each direction holds one outer lane. Nothing in the message says which direction goes where.

The first four runs start from the same evenly spaced state and show the first 100 rounds; the others are shown in full. Every video is a single run. The counts for random starts are from 13 runs of one model: they are observations, not statistics.

What each bird is told

This is the complete message one bird receives for one move. It says what the bird sees, the rules and the goal, and nothing else: no history, no position on the ring, no name.

The runs with and without the die differ only by the sentence marked like this. Whether to roll, and with which weights, is left to the bird.

Example: a bird in an outer lane that meets one other bird one cell apart, with nothing else in view.

You are a bird on a track of square cells with three lanes, A, B and C, that run side by side: lane B lies between lane A and lane C. A cell holds at most one bird.
Cells are named by lane and by offset along the track from your own cell, which is A 0. Offset +1 is the next cell on the side you are facing, offset -1 is the next cell on the opposite side, and so on.
A bird can stay where it is or move one cell toward the side it faces, ending in its own lane or in a lane next to its own. Your moves are named by the lane they end in:
A: to A +1
B: to B +1
The wind you feel right now blows toward the + side.
A bird faces the way the wind it feels blows. Each bird sees only the cells around itself and chooses its own move.
You can see the cells with offsets from -2 to +2 in all three lanes. Right now:
A +2: a bird facing the - side
A +1: empty
A -1: empty
A -2: empty
B +2: empty
B +1: empty
B 0: empty
B -1: empty
B -2: empty
C +2: empty
C +1: empty
C 0: empty
C -1: empty
C -2: empty
A move into a cell that is occupied does not happen. If two birds try to move into the same cell, neither of them moves. If the moves of two birds would cross each other, neither of them moves.
Rule: a move that does not happen counts as one clash for the bird that tried it. The birds' shared goal is that every bird travels as smoothly as it can along the wind it feels, with as few clashes as possible.
A tool named draw is available: give it a weight for any of A, B and Stay (an option you leave out gets weight 0), and it returns one of them drawn at random with probability proportional to the weights. You may use it or not.
Now choose your move.
No need to explain. End your final reply with a line that contains only "Answer:" followed by A, B or Stay.

Lane names carry no information: the names of the three lanes and the order in which they are listed are drawn anew for every bird in every round. All prompts, word for word, are in PROMPTS.md.

Nothing sets which lane carries which direction. Different runs settle into different, mirror-image lanes: order that no bird was told to make.
